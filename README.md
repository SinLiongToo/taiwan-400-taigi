# 台灣四百年・台語旁白 (Taiwan: Four Centuries, narrated in Taiwanese)

The 400-year animated history page from the `taiwan-400` project
(<https://sinliongtoo.github.io/taiwan-400/>), with a spoken Taiwanese (Taigi) narration added.
The map, charts, rulers and 中文 / EN / 中英 text are unchanged; this copy adds the voice.

**Live pages**

- 台灣四百年・台語旁白 — <https://sinliongtoo.github.io/taiwan-400-taigi/>
- 二二八與白色恐怖・台語旁白 — <https://sinliongtoo.github.io/taiwan-400-taigi/228.html>

Feature pages with the same narration, read-along subtitles and pictures (all under the same address):
`dutch.html` 荷西時期, `guo.html` 郭懷一事件, `koxinga.html` 鄭成功攻台, `feuds.html` 清代分類械鬥,
`linshuangwen.html` 林爽文事件, `daichaochun.html` 戴潮春事件, `yiwei.html` 乙未戰爭,
`indigenous.html` 原住民族抗日, `tapani.html` 西來庵事件, `wushe.html` 霧社事件, `airraids.html` 美軍大空襲.
Only the English guide (`intro.html`) still opens on the original site.

`missionaries.html` 四位傳教士 (Four Missionaries, 1865–1935: Maxwell, Mackay, Campbell, Barclay) is built the same
way as `poj.html` below, with events coloured by person and a short life of each; script `tools/narration_missionaries.json`.

`poj.html` 白話字的歷史 (The Story of Pe̍h-ōe-jī, 1832–2006) is a page written for this site, not copied
from `taiwan-400`: a timeline film of 25 milestones in seven periods, with the wartime and post-war bans
marked in red, a list of the bans, notes on where sources disagree, and a Sources section. It is a
complete page (edit it directly); its narration script is `tools/narration_poj.json`.

The 228 page has two films (the 228 Incident day by day, the White Terror year by year). They share
one voice: starting one film pauses the other. With narration on, the films wait for 播放 instead of
starting by themselves when scrolled into view.

## What the narration does

- Press 播放. Each era text and each event is read aloud in Taigi as the film reaches it.
- The years keep moving while a passage is spoken, but wait just before the next one until the
  voice has finished. A full run takes about as long as the narration (see the minutes shown
  next to the record button) instead of one minute.
- The line being spoken is shown under the film in 台語漢字 with 白話字 (POJ) below it.
- Read-along highlight: the character being spoken turns gold, characters already spoken are bright,
  and the rest stay dim. The POJ of the phrase being spoken is brightened too.
  Each phrase (the text between punctuation marks) starts and ends exactly with the voice; inside a
  phrase the characters share the time evenly, so a single character can be a fraction of a second early or late.
- 🔊 台語旁白 turns the voice off; the film then plays at its original speed. The choice is remembered.
- Jumping to an era, dragging the slider or pressing ← → stops the voice and picks it up from the new year.
- 錄製影片 records the narration into the video when the page is opened over http(s).
  Opened as a local file (`file://`), browsers only allow the picture to be recorded.

## Pictures under the films

Beside the narration line (under it on phones) each film shows one historical picture for the moment
on screen, with a caption and a credit that links to its Wikimedia Commons page: 16 on the main page
(from Dapper's 1670 engraving to Taipei today), 6 for the 228 Incident and 9 for the White Terror.
Most are public domain; five are Creative Commons and are credited by author and licence.

To change them, edit `tools/pictures.json` (`t` = when the picture starts showing, `file` = the exact
file name on Commons, plus the two captions) and run `python tools/fetch_images.py`. It downloads
into `images/` and rewrites `pictures.js`; files that are missing or not freely licensed are skipped.
The pictures are part of the page, not the film, so they are not in a recorded video.

## How the feature pages are wired

The eleven feature pages are copies from `taiwan-400` with one shared module, `tg.js`, installed by
`python tools/add_narration.py <path to taiwan-400> <page> ...`. The module adds the narration button,
the subtitle box and the picture panel, and makes the page's clock wait for the voice. Each page has

- `tools/narration_<page>.json` (Taigi script) -> `narration-<page>.js` and `audio/<page>/*.mp3`,
  made with `python tools/make_narration.py --script tools/narration_<page>.json`;
- a list in `tools/pictures.json` under the page's name, where `at` is the clip at which a picture appears.

Clip ids are positions, not dates: `p0`, `p1`, ... for the page's phase texts and `e0`, `e1`, ... for its
events, in the order they appear in the page. If `taiwan-400` adds or reorders events, renumber the script.
The five pages built on the shared rebellion engine (guo, linshuangwen, daichaochun, feuds, indigenous)
have one opening passage (`p0`) instead of one per phase. `tools/narration_common.json` holds
pronunciation fixes shared by all feature pages.

To pick up a changed page from `taiwan-400`, run `tools/add_narration.py` again for that page; it stops
with a message if the page's playback code no longer matches what it expects.

## Changing the narration

1. Edit `tools/narration_script.json`.
   - `han` is the Taigi text, written in 教育部推薦用字. It is what the subtitle shows.
   - `lexicon` replaces words the romanizer misreads with Tâi-lô before the text is spoken
     (names, years read digit by digit, words like 雞籠 → `Ke-lâng`). Add an entry when a word sounds wrong.
   - A clip id must match a year in the page: `era-<start year>` for an era text, `ev-<event year>` for an event.
     An event without a clip is simply not read (1875, 1937.6, 1949.38 and 1987.54 are covered by their era text).
2. `python tools/make_narration.py --dry-run` prints the romanization so it can be checked before any audio is made.
3. `python tools/make_narration.py` synthesizes only the clips whose text changed, then rewrites `narration.js`.

For the 228 page, edit `tools/narration_228.json` and add `--script tools/narration_228.json` to both commands.
Its clip ids are `p228-<phase number>`, `e228-<month-day>`, `pwt-<phase number>` and `ewt-<year>`.

The voice is Meta MMS-TTS (`facebook/mms-tts-nan`), run through the engine in the Taigi TTS Studio
project (`../Projects_antigravity/TIGI_TTS_MOREＭＯＤＬ`; pass `--engine-dir` if it moves). It needs
`torch`, `transformers`, `taibun`, `scipy` and `ffmpeg`.

## Limits

- The voice is a synthetic one. It is real Taiwanese, not Mandarin, but its tones are not always natural,
  and proper names follow the readings in the lexicon.
- The Taigi script is a translation of the page's Chinese text and has not been reviewed by a second speaker.
- History content, sources and portrait credits are those of the `taiwan-400` project; see the References on the page.
